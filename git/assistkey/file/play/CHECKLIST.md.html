1 # Publishing AssistKey on Google Play
2 
3 Everything that can be prepared without your Google account is already in this
4 repository. What is left needs your login, your card, or your identity, in this
5 order.
6 
7 ## 1. Open the developer account (you, once)
8 
9 1. Go to <https://play.google.com/console/signup> with the Google account that
10    should own the app.
11 2. Choose **Personal** unless you have a registered business.
12 3. Pay the registration fee: **US$25, one time**. (Not $5.)
13 4. Complete identity verification. Google asks for a government ID and a phone
14    number, and the name must match the payment card. Approval takes from a few
15    hours to a few days.
16 5. To be paid, set up a **payments profile** (Play Console > Setup > Payments
17    profile) with a bank account and tax information. You cannot create a priced
18    product until this exists.
19 
20 A personal account created after November 2023 must run a **closed test with at
21 least 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days** before Google will let the
22 app go to production. That is your beta; plan for it.
23 
24 ## 2. Create the app
25 
26 Play Console > **Create app**
27 
28 | Field | Value |
29 |---|---|
30 | App name | `AssistKey: E-Ink Key Remap` |
31 | Default language | English (United States) |
32 | App or game | App |
33 | Free or paid | **Free** (the licence is an in-app product; a paid app could not give testers a discount) |
34 
35 The package name is fixed by the first upload: `dev.equwal.assistkey`.
36 
37 ## 3. App signing - do this before the first upload
38 
39 Play re-signs every app. If it signs with a key of its own, the APK you hand to
40 testers and the copy Play delivers will carry different signatures. Then Play
41 cannot upgrade a sideloaded install in place, and purchases made from a
42 sideloaded install fail.
43 
44 So give Play **the key this repo already signs with**:
45 
46 Play Console > Test and release > Setup > **App signing** > *Use a different
47 key* > *Export and upload a key from Java keystore*. Download the
48 `encryption_public_key.pem` it offers and the PEPK tool, then:
49 
50 ```bash
51 java -jar pepk.jar --keystore=assistkey-release.jks --alias=assistkey \
52   --output=assistkey-play-signing.zip --include-cert \
53   --rsa-aes-encryption --encryption-key-path=encryption_public_key.pem
54 ```
55 
56 It asks for the keystore password, which is in `keystore.properties`. Upload the
57 zip. Expected certificate SHA-256:
58 
59 ```
60 bcb3127e1931a9b906e8e2498184ef5380f97cbf3531e5bc8bb0064476ee2919
61 ```
62 
63 **Back up `assistkey-release.jks` and `keystore.properties` somewhere safe.**
64 Neither is in git. Without them you cannot ship another update.
65 
66 ## 4. Upload the build
67 
68 Test and release > Testing > **Internal testing** > Create new release > upload
69 `AssistKey-<version>-play.aab` (attached to the GitHub release, or rebuild with
70 `./gradlew bundlePlayRelease`). Upload the `play` build only. It has no Shizuku
71 code and no Shizuku permission. The `full` APK is for direct install.
72 
73 Internal testing needs no review and is live in minutes. Products cannot be
74 created until one build has been uploaded.
75 
76 ## 5. Create the three products
77 
78 Monetize with Play > Products > **One-time products**. The ids can never be
79 changed or reused, and the app looks for exactly these:
80 
81 | Product id | Name | Price | State |
82 |---|---|---|---|
83 | `assistkey_pro` | AssistKey licence | **US$2.99** | Active |
84 | `assistkey_pro_tester` | AssistKey licence - beta tester price | **US$1.49** | Active |
85 | `assistkey_beta_open` | Beta access flag (not for sale) | US$0.99 | **Active while the beta runs** |
86 
87 All three: purchase type **Buy**, non-consumable. Let Play convert the prices
88 to other currencies.
89 
90 `assistkey_beta_open` is never offered by the app. It is only a switch - see
91 section 9.
92 
93 Price review, 2026-09-21: the market check advises **US$4.99**, not US$2.99.
94 Reason: US$2.99 is the exact price of Button Mapper Pro, which has 5M+ installs,
95 so a price match cannot win; the e-ink features have no paid competitor. At
96 US$4.99 the margin is US$4.24 per sale, and US$1,000 a month needs 236 sales
97 and not 394. The owner has not decided. A price is a Console edit, not a release.
98 
99 Pricing: Button Mapper Pro, the nearest comparable app, is US$2.99. AssistKey
100 serves a far smaller audience but does more on the hardware it targets, so it
101 matches that price rather than undercutting it. Prices are read from Play at
102 run time; changing one is a Console edit, not a release.

120 ## 8. Test the purchase before anyone else does
121 
122 Billing could not be tested before the app existed on Play. Do this first:
123 
124 1. Play Console > Setup > **License testing**: add your own Gmail address.
125    License testers buy with a test card and are never charged.
126 2. Internal testing > Testers: add the same address, open the opt-in link on
127    the reader, install from Play.
128 3. On the reader, Google Play must be switched on - the Viwoods firmware ships
129    with it disabled.
130 4. Open AssistKey > the licence row. Prices should appear within a second or
131    two. Buy with the test card and confirm it shows **Unlocked**.
132 5. Refund it from Order management to test again.
133 
134 If the prices never appear, the usual causes are: products not Active, the
135 account not on the tester list, or the signatures not matching (section 3).
136 
137 ## 9. Running the beta, and ending it
138 
139 **During the beta** hand testers either the Play opt-in link or
140 the `play` APK from the GitHub release. Both are the same build. While
141 `assistkey_beta_open` is Active, every install is fully unlocked for free, and
142 each one quietly marks itself as a tester.
143 
144 **To end the beta:** set `assistkey_beta_open` to **Inactive**. Within hours,
145 every install that can reach Play drops to a 7-day trial and then locks.
146 Installs that cannot reach Play lock on the date compiled into the build,
147 `2027-03-31` (`assistkey.betaExpires` in `gradle.properties`).
148 
149 **The tester discount** then appears by itself: an install that ran during the
150 beta is offered `assistkey_pro_tester` instead of `assistkey_pro`. A tester who
151 changed device, or had to reinstall, types the tester code instead. The code is
152 in `keystore.properties` (`testerCode`), never in git; only its hash ships.
153 Changing it means a new release.
154 
155 To stop offering the discount later, deactivate `assistkey_pro_tester`.
156 
157 ## 10. Go to production
158 
159 After the 14-day closed test, Play Console offers **Apply for production
160 access**. It asks about the test; answer plainly. Then promote the build to
161 Production and choose countries.
