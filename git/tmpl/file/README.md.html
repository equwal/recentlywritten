3 Templates for all kinds of things. Just read a file into your editor at the cursor and jump to the `<++>` locations.
4 
5 A [therealtruex.com](https://therealtruex.com) project.
6 
7 vim config:
8 ```
9 map <leader><Space> /<CR><++><BS>
10 ```
11 
12 Here is one such template:
13 ```
14 (defpackage :<++>-system (:use :cl :asdf))
15 (in-package :<++>-system)
16 (defsystem <++>
17   :name "<++>"
18   :author "<++> <<++>@<++>.<++>>"
19   :version "<++>"
20   :maintainer "<++> <<++>@<++>.<++>>"
21   :license "GNU GPLv3.0"
22   :description "<++>"
23   ;; put the most general files first to minimize the "depends on" use
24   ;; and in that case you'll need :serial t
25   :serial <++>
26   :components ((:file "packages")
27                (:file "<++>" :depends-on ("<++>")))
28   :weakly-depends-on (<++>)
29   :depends-on (<++>))
30 ```

38 
39 Make sure to add a templating shortcut to your leader key to jump to
40 the `<++>` spots.
41 ```vim
42     -- Templating shortcut
43     map("n", "<leader><Space>", "/<++><CR>dt>a<BS>", { desc = "Jump to next template placeholder" })
44 ```
