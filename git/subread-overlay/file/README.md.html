1 # SubRead Overlay
2 
3 Shows the lines of a subtitle file (`.srt`) over the app that plays your
4 audiobook or video. The line follows the position of the player. Tap a word to
5 select it, and send it to a dictionary.
6 
7 It works with each Android player that publishes a media session: Voice, VLC,
8 mpv-android, YouTube, Smart AudioBook Player, podcast apps. No player needs a
9 change, and the app has no network permission.
10 
11 No `.srt` for your audiobook? [SubRead](https://subread.space) makes one from
12 the audiobook and its ebook, in the browser or on
13 [Android](https://github.com/equwal/subread-android).

24 ## How to use it
25 
26 1. Allow "Show over other apps".
27 2. Allow notification access. Android gives the position of other apps only to
28    an app with this access. The app uses it for the media position, the speed,
29    and play or pause. It does not read, keep or send a notification: the
30    listener has no `onNotificationPosted`.
31 3. Choose the `.srt` file.
32 4. Choose the dictionary for "Look up", or let Android ask each time. Each app
33    that has an entry in the text selection menu is in the list (Takoboto,
34    Aedict, AnkiDroid, a translator).
35 5. Press "Show the subtitles", then start the player.
36 
37 Two settings change the panel: how much of the player shows through it, and
38 whether it shows the line before and the line after the line of now.
39 
40 On the panel:
41 
42 - `≡` moves the panel.
43 - A tap on a word pauses the player at once and selects the word. A drag
44   selects more words. When the finger lifts, the selection goes to the
45   dictionary. "Share" opens the share sheet of Android, for an app without an entry in
46   the text selection menu. "Copy" copies it.
47 - `▶` starts the player again after a lookup. `⏸` pauses it.
48 - `⋯` opens the timing row. `−0.5 s` and `+0.5 s` shift the subtitles.
49   `◀ line` and `line ▶` make the line before, or the next line, the line of
50   now. Use them when the media has an intro that the subtitle file does not
51   have.
52 - `✕` closes the panel.
53 
54 The line stays on the panel until the next line starts, also in a silence, so
55 that there is time to look a word up.
56 
57 ## How the timing works
58 
59 A media session reports a position, the time of that report, and the speed.
60 `PlayClock` computes the position of now from these three. `Follower` sleeps
61 until the next line starts and wakes on each report of the player (pause,
62 seek, speed). It does not poll.
63 
64 A player that reports no position: Android counts from zero when that player
65 starts to play, and the panel holds its line in a pause. Set the timing with
66 `◀ line` and `line ▶`.
67 
68 The app does not use an accessibility service, and will not. Players that
69 hide their position (Netflix, some DRM players) are not supported.
70 
71 ## Anki cards
72 
73 With [SubRead Anki](https://github.com/equwal/subread-anki) installed, the
74 selection row has an "Anki" button. One tap makes a card in AnkiDroid: the
75 word, its reading and definition (from SubRead Dictionary), the line as the
76 sentence, a screenshot of the player, the word audio and the line read by the
77 voice of the device. The panel hides for a moment so that the screenshot shows
78 the player. SubRead Anki has its own optional capture service; this app stays
79 without one.
80 
81 A book in many audio files: the player reports the position in the current
82 file, and the subtitle file has one clock for the whole book. Shift the
83 subtitles with the timing row at the start of each file. One `.m4b` for one
84 book has no such problem.

86 ## For reader apps
87 
88 A reader app has no notification access, so it cannot see the position of
89 the player. This app answers for it, with a content provider at
90 `content://space.subread.overlay.player/state`. A query returns one row with
91 the column `state`:
92 
93 ```
94 playing=1;position=96153;speed=1.0;package=de.ph1b.audiobook
95 ```
96 
97 The position is in milliseconds, for the moment of the query. A problem is
98 `error=no_notification_access` or `error=no_player`. `call` with the method
99 `play`, `pause` or `seek` (the argument is the position in milliseconds)
100 controls the player. The panel does not need to be on the screen.
101 
102 The [SubRead plugin for KOReader](https://github.com/equwal/subread.koplugin)
103 uses this to turn the pages with the audiobook.
104 
105 ## For flash card apps
106 
107 A query of `content://space.subread.overlay.player/line` returns one row: the
108 subtitle line of now, with the report of the player as it came, so that a
109 flash card app can put the line on a card and cut its sound. The columns:
110 
111 - `state`: the same line as `/state`.
112 - `reported_position`, `reported_at`, `speed`, `playing`: the last report of
113   the player. `reported_at` is on the clock of `SystemClock.elapsedRealtime()`.
114   A pause reported a minute ago still maps each time of the media to the
115   moment it played.
116 - `offset`: the shift the user set. A time of the subtitle file is `offset`
117   milliseconds later than the same moment of the player.
118 - `index`, `start`, `end`, `text`, `before`, `after`: the line. `index` is -1
119   before the first line, and `text` is then null. The times are on the clock
120   of the subtitle file.
121 
122 The query parameter `position` (milliseconds, on the clock of the player)
123 picks the line for that position in place of the position of now. The panel
124 does not need to be on the screen, but the user must have chosen the `.srt`.
125 
126 [SubRead Anki](https://github.com/equwal/subread-anki) uses this. Choose it
127 as the dictionary in step 4: a tap on a word then makes a card with the line,
128 its sound and a picture of the screen.
129 
130 ## For caption apps
131 
132 An app that makes captions from live audio, for example speech recognition of
133 the sound of a video, can show its lines on the panel. The user then taps the
134 words and looks them up, the same as with a subtitle file. The app calls the
135 same content provider:
136 
137 ```kotlin
138 val panel = Uri.parse("content://space.subread.overlay.player")
139 contentResolver.call(panel, "line", "It was a dark", bundleOf("partial" to true))
140 contentResolver.call(panel, "line", "It was a dark night.", null)
141 contentResolver.call(panel, "end", null, null)
142 ```
143 
144 `line` shows the text. The extra `partial` is true while the sentence goes on:
145 the panel adds `…` to the line, and the next line replaces it. A final line
146 (no `partial`) stays as the line before, when the user shows three lines. `end`
147 gives the panel back to the subtitle file. Without `end`, live lines end ten
148 minutes after the last one.
149 
150 The answer is in the bundle key `live`: `ok`, or the reason the panel cannot
151 show the line. `no_notification_access` and `no_overlay_permission`: the user
152 must allow steps 1 and 2. `panel_hidden`: the user must press "Show the
153 subtitles", or closed the panel with `✕`. The panel does not come back on its
154 own for a line, so that a close stays a close.
155 
156 While a word is selected, the panel holds the line, so that the lookup has
157 time. The newest line comes when the selection goes.
158 
159 From a shell, for a test:
160 
161 ```
162 adb shell content call --uri content://space.subread.overlay.player --method line --arg "It was a dark" --extra partial:b:true
163 ```

165 ## Build
166 
167 ```
168 ./gradlew :core:test :app:assembleDebug
169 ./gradlew :app:connectedDebugAndroidTest   # word selection and the follower, on a device
170 ```
171 
172 `:core` is plain Kotlin: the subtitle reader, the line for a position, the
173 clock. It has property tests. `:app` has the panel and the listener.
174 
175 `-PplayStore=true` leaves the Ko-fi link out of the build for Google Play.
